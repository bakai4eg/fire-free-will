## Project Name
BurnBeat — a harmonized fire calendar for any place on Earth

## Summary
BurnBeat turns 25 years of NASA satellite fire detections into one consistent record and a simple tool. MODIS (2000–) and VIIRS (2012–) see fire differently: VIIRS detects about four times more small fires, so the raw record shows a false jump from 4.4 to 17.4 million hotspots a year in 2012. We harmonized the two sensors by converting MODIS to the VIIRS scale with a ratio for every 1° cell and month, fitted on 2012–2016 before MODIS orbit drift, and validated it by hiding each year 2012–2019 in turn: bias −0.3% instead of −75%, monthly error 19× smaller. Kyrgyzstan is processed at 0.25° with individual hotspots and real region borders.

The web app answers three questions for any place on Earth (search, click a country or draw an area): when does it usually burn (a burning calendar 2001–2025 with hotspots, anomaly, burned extent and rainfall), is it worse than normal right now (last 7 days, refreshed every 3 hours, against a VIIRS-era normal), and how risky are the next 3 days (season, anomaly and weather forecast). A Telegram bot sends Kyrgyz emergency services, local governments and farmers a daily fire-danger brief. This helps responders plan the season and act before fires spread, not after.

## Project Details
WHAT IT DOES
BurnBeat answers three questions for any place on Earth: When does it usually burn? Is it worse than normal right now? How risky are the next 3 days? Users search for a place, click a country, or draw their own area on the map.

HOW IT WORKS
1. Harmonization. NASA FIRMS archives of MODIS C6.1 (2000–2025) and VIIRS S-NPP (2012–2025) are filtered to vegetation fires (factories, gas flares and volcanoes removed). MODIS is converted to the VIIRS scale with a VIIRS/MODIS ratio for every 1° cell and calendar month, fitted on 2012–2016 (before MODIS orbit drift), smoothed and shrunk toward regional ratios. The Terra-only months of 2000–2002 are corrected. Validation hides each year 2012–2019 in turn: bias −0.3% (raw MODIS −75%), monthly error 19× smaller. Kyrgyzstan is processed at 0.25° with individual hotspots and real region borders.
2. Burning calendar 2001–2025 with four views: hotspots, anomaly vs normal, burned extent (share of the area with fire) and rainfall (NASA POWER). A 25-year playback fills the calendar month by month.
3. "Now vs normal": last 7 days of VIIRS NOAA-20/21 near-real-time data, refreshed every 3 hours on our server, compared with a VIIRS-era normal.
4. Fire danger for 3 days: season, anomaly, fresh detections and the Open-Meteo forecast (heat, humidity, wind, rain). It is a rating of conditions, not a fire forecast.
5. Villages near fires (Kyrgyzstan) and a Telegram bot that sends a daily brief to emergency services, local governments and farmers.

BENEFITS AND IMPACT
Fire managers can finally compare this season with the past honestly, see anomalies early, plan patrols before the peak, and warn the people who light agricultural fires, not only those who put them out. Findings: wet months burn noticeably less in every Kyrgyz region (r ≈ −0.4).

TOOLS
Python (data pipeline, no heavy dependencies), JavaScript with d3 and ECharts (web app), Google Cloud VM with Caddy, GitHub Pages and GitHub Actions, aiogram (Telegram bot). Data: NASA FIRMS, NASA POWER, Open-Meteo, GeoNames, geoBoundaries. Three languages: English, Russian, Kyrgyz.

WHAT WE CONSIDERED
Scientific honesty (published method, validation and limits: ~100 km grid outside Kyrgyzstan, 2001 seen by one satellite), accessible colours, mobile use, speed (loads in under a second), and local users who need plain language rather than charts.
